# Family animation research — 4 November 2026

This is development research, not in-world copy. Techniques only: no downloaded models, textures, animation clips, source code, or new runtime dependencies. Implementation remains original JavaScript and procedural solid meshes in three.js r128.

## What is worth taking from shipped games

**A small number of strong poses can carry a performance.** David Rosen's GDC session discusses procedural animation in *Overgrowth*, *Receiver* and *Black Shades*, spanning first-person and third-person presentation. The useful budget lesson is to spend authoring effort on readable poses and the interpolation between them, then use runtime constraints for contact. Our application: retain the existing shared skeleton, FK/IK and atlas; improve the most frequently seen transitions before adding more actions. [GDC session, Rosen / Wolfire](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020583/Animation-Bootcamp-An-Indie-Approach).

**Predict contact rather than chasing the floor.** Ubisoft's foot-IK work contrasts reactive correction with predictive placement: reacting late produces either lag or knee snaps. Our adaptation is a world-space stance target, a separate swing trajectory, clearance before a stair riser and a landing target chosen while the foot is airborne. Never smooth the planted foot toward the root. Lower the pelvis when a leg cannot reach. Sample actual stair treads, not just the ramp used by navigation. [Roche and Torres-Cros, GDC 2016](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023316/Fitting-the-World-A-Biomechanical).

**Locomotion includes its boundaries.** The animation-driven locomotion presentation distinguishes starting, moving, turning and stopping, with foot-contact information at transitions. It also describes the compromises of a shipped crowd system: an appropriate limited solution can beat a costly general one. For us, keep pathfinding and collision authoritative; infer actual displacement for lateral steps, finish an airborne foot when stopping, and move only one foot at a time during a stationary turn. The body should transfer weight toward the supporting foot. Do not rewrite navigation as part of a rig polish pass. [Anguelov et al., GDC 2012](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2012/slides/Summit_AI/Anguelov_Bobby_AnimationDrivenLocomotion.pdf).

**Polish comes from inspecting transitions in context.** Naughty Dog's presentation describes previs, prototyping, feedback and seamless transitions as parts of its animation workflow. Our budget cannot reproduce its content volume, but can reproduce the iteration discipline: identical camera, lighting and animation timestamps before/after; front, profile and rear; 2 m and 4 m; real jobs as well as a neutral rig probe. [Yates and Soria, GDC 2017](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024309/Animation-Bootcamp-Uncharted-4-Naughty).

## IK for feet, stairs, hands and props

Two-bone IK solves an elbow or knee from an end target and bend direction. It is suitable for foot contacts on uneven ground and hands on moving objects. For this rig, targets must be expressed in its y-up centimetres, then converted consistently from house y-down space. Keep anatomical bend poles and clamp reachable distances. A wrist target alone is insufficient: the palm orientation and the prop's grip offset must agree. Paper needs two grip points; a mug needs a hand-relative handle; a door requires a world-space handle target that follows its hinge, followed by release. [Epic's IK setup documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/ik-setups?application_version=4.27).

Original design proposal: during stair swing, gain height early going up and delay descent until over the tread going down; keep the sole clear of intermediate risers. Choose heel/toe roll around the appropriate contact point, rather than raising the whole shoe through the tread. During sitting, feet stay available as supports while the hips go back and down; standing reverses the weight transfer, with a forward torso lean before hip rise. Retain the last seat descriptor through the stand transition so clearing `seat` cannot instantly restore straight legs. These are proposed applications, not claims that any cited game uses our exact formulas.

Sitnichenko's sitting demonstration breaks the action into standing, leaning, making contact and settling. That is a useful small pose budget: the torso must begin its forward counterbalance before the pelvis completes its descent, then relax after contact. The same continuous phase should survive interruption halfway down or up; do not restart an animation on every repeated state assignment. [Animation Mentor sitting demonstration](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-animate-characters-sitting-down/).

## Secondary motion and idle variation

Breath should move the ribcage and shoulders gently, rather than scale the whole character. Use different phase and a small frequency variation per person; suppress large gestures during precise tasks. An occasional head adjustment or asymmetric elbow relaxation reads better than constant whole-body noise. [Martinsen's breathing demonstration, Animation Mentor](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-animate-natural-breathing-loops/).

Hair and loose clothing should lag their attachment and settle after a stop. A bounded damped response on existing hair bones is enough for bunches; Mom's lower bob can share those bones with a small head-weight blend. Cloth folds belong in the model's silhouette and atlas, with motion inherited from existing body bones. Full cloth simulation is inappropriate here. Secondary motion is a mechanical response; secondary action is acting that supports the main action, such as a small thoughtful glance. [Seymour's secondary-motion demonstration](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-how-to-animate-secondary-motion/), [Animation Mentor on secondary action](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/secondary-action-the-12-basic-principles-of-animation/).

## Eyes, head and expression

Disney Research's gaze architecture layers attention, habituation, saccades and motor responses with different speeds. It concerns an animatronic, not a game; the transferable principle is that eyes react quickly while the head follows more slowly. Here, atlas UV offsets can carry the residual angle between the requested gaze and the *current* head orientation. Small irregular saccades and occasional brief gaze aversion prevent staring. Blinks should close rapidly, spend little time shut, then reopen through the existing half-eye frame. Do not add realistic eyeballs or eyelashes to this deliberately simple face. [Pan et al., Disney Research, 2020](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/realistic-and-interactive-robot-gaze/).

## The twelve principles as an engineering checklist

Use **squash and stretch** sparingly through joint compression, preserving anatomy; **anticipation** before reaching/sitting; **staging** through clear hand/prop silhouettes; **straight-ahead and pose-to-pose** as a combination of responsive secondary motion and authored task poses; **follow-through and overlap** for hair and arms; **slow in/out** for transitions, never to slide a planted foot; **arcs** for swings and reaches; **secondary action** for thought and attention; **timing** for weight and age; **exaggeration** only enough to read at gameplay distance; **solid drawing** as solid forms and coherent balance; **appeal** through consistent friendly shapes. A teaching source is Disney veteran Jay Jackson's demonstration. This checklist is our application to the rig. [Animation Mentor workshop](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/free-workshop-12-principles-of-animation/).

## Modelling and shading on a phone budget

Valve's character art emphasizes distinctive silhouettes, controlled detail and readable shading. Translate that to Mom's asymmetric bob, Dad's fuller jaw and side part, and Crystal's parted hair and tapered bunches. Shape the cranium, temple, cheek and chin in the existing head surface; retain painted features. Reallocate existing hand vertices to a palm, finger grouping and visible thumb gap. Shape existing hair vertices into broad overlapping locks rather than adding strand meshes. Use large, low-contrast colour changes following those forms, avoiding noisy alternating stripes. Reserve tiny detail for close views. [Mitchell, Francke and Eng, Valve's illustrative-rendering slides](https://cdn.cloudflare.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2_Slides.pdf).

For shading, keep smooth normals across connected head/hair forms and explicit creases at a sole or cuff; close all surfaces. Geometry changes must preserve every outfit and both LODs. The existing one skinned mesh plus shadow stays at two draws. Low/Potato must remain below 3,000 triangles including shadows, which is stricter than the requested +25% cap. Benchmark warmed rig updates separately from software-GPU frame time, and report the host's limitations. No extra per-frame mesh construction, texture uploads, lights or libraries.
