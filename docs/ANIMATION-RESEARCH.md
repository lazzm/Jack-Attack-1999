# Family animation research — 4 November 2026

This is development research, not in-world copy. Techniques only: no downloaded models, textures, animation clips, source code, or new runtime dependencies. Implementation remains original JavaScript and procedural solid meshes in three.js r128.

## What is worth taking from shipped games

**A small number of strong poses can carry a performance.** David Rosen's GDC session discusses procedural animation in *Overgrowth*, *Receiver* and *Black Shades*, spanning first-person and third-person presentation. The useful budget lesson is to spend authoring effort on readable poses and the interpolation between them, then use runtime constraints for contact. Our application: retain the existing shared skeleton, FK/IK and atlas; improve the most frequently seen transitions before adding more actions. [GDC session, Rosen / Wolfire](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020583/Animation-Bootcamp-An-Indie-Approach).

**Predict contact rather than chasing the floor.** Ubisoft's foot-IK work contrasts reactive correction with predictive placement: reacting late produces either lag or knee snaps. Our adaptation is a world-space stance target, a separate swing trajectory, clearance before a stair riser and a landing target chosen while the foot is airborne. Never smooth the planted foot toward the root. Lower the pelvis when a leg cannot reach. Sample actual stair treads, not just the ramp used by navigation. [Roche and Torres-Cros, GDC 2016](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023316/Fitting-the-World-A-Biomechanical).

**Locomotion includes its boundaries.** The animation-driven locomotion presentation distinguishes starting, moving, turning and stopping, with foot-contact information at transitions. It also describes the compromises of a shipped crowd system: an appropriate limited solution can beat a costly general one. For us, keep pathfinding and collision authoritative; infer actual displacement for lateral steps, finish an airborne foot when stopping, and move only one foot at a time during a stationary turn. The body should transfer weight toward the supporting foot. Do not rewrite navigation as part of a rig polish pass. [Anguelov et al., GDC 2012](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2012/slides/Summit_AI/Anguelov_Bobby_AnimationDrivenLocomotion.pdf).

**Polish comes from inspecting transitions in context.** Naughty Dog's presentation describes previs, prototyping, feedback and seamless transitions as parts of its animation workflow. Our budget cannot reproduce its content volume, but can reproduce the iteration discipline: identical camera, lighting and animation timestamps before/after; front, profile and rear; 2 m and 4 m; real jobs as well as a neutral rig probe. [Yates and Soria, GDC 2017](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024309/Animation-Bootcamp-Uncharted-4-Naughty).

## IK for feet, stairs, hands and props

Two-bone IK solves an elbow or knee from an end target and bend direction. It is suitable for foot contacts on uneven ground and hands on moving objects. For this rig, targets must be expressed in its y-up centimetres, then converted consistently from house y-down space. Keep anatomical bend poles and clamp reachable distances. A wrist target alone is insufficient: the palm orientation and the prop's grip offset must agree. Paper needs two grip points; a mug needs a hand-relative handle; a door requires a world-space handle target that follows its hinge, followed by release. [Epic's IK setup documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/ik-setups?application_version=4.27).

Original design proposal: during stair swing, gain height early going up and delay descent until over the tread going down; keep the sole clear of intermediate risers. Choose heel/toe roll around the appropriate contact point, rather than raising the whole shoe through the tread. During sitting, feet stay available as supports while the hips go back and down; standing reverses the weight transfer, with a forward torso lean before hip rise. Retain the last seat descriptor through the stand transition so clearing `seat` cannot instantly restore straight legs. These are proposed applications, not claims that any cited game uses our exact formulas.

Sitnichenko's sitting demonstration breaks the action into standing, leaning, making contact and settling. That is a useful small pose budget: the torso must begin its forward counterbalance before the pelvis completes its descent, then relax after contact. The same continuous phase should survive interruption halfway down or up; do not restart an animation on every repeated state assignment. [Animation Mentor sitting demonstration](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-animate-characters-sitting-down/).

## Secondary motion and idle variation

Breath should move the ribcage and shoulders gently, rather than scale the whole character. Use different phase and a small frequency variation per person; suppress large gestures during precise tasks. An occasional head adjustment or asymmetric elbow relaxation reads better than constant whole-body noise. [Martinsen's breathing demonstration, Animation Mentor](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-animate-natural-breathing-loops/).

Hair and loose clothing should lag their attachment and settle after a stop. A bounded damped response on existing hair bones is enough for bunches; Mom's lower bob can share those bones with a small head-weight blend. Cloth folds belong in the model's silhouette and atlas, with motion inherited from existing body bones. Full cloth simulation is inappropriate here. Secondary motion is a mechanical response; secondary action is acting that supports the main action, such as a small thoughtful glance. [Seymour's secondary-motion demonstration](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-how-to-animate-secondary-motion/), [Animation Mentor on secondary action](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/secondary-action-the-12-basic-principles-of-animation/).

## Eyes, head and expression

Disney Research's gaze architecture layers attention, habituation, saccades and motor responses with different speeds. It concerns an animatronic, not a game; the transferable principle is that eyes react quickly while the head follows more slowly. Here, atlas UV offsets can carry the residual angle between the requested gaze and the *current* head orientation. Small irregular saccades and occasional brief gaze aversion prevent staring. Blinks should close rapidly, spend little time shut, then reopen through the existing half-eye frame. Do not add realistic eyeballs or eyelashes to this deliberately simple face. [Pan et al., Disney Research, 2020](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/realistic-and-interactive-robot-gaze/).

## The twelve principles as an engineering checklist

Use **squash and stretch** sparingly through joint compression, preserving anatomy; **anticipation** before reaching/sitting; **staging** through clear hand/prop silhouettes; **straight-ahead and pose-to-pose** as a combination of responsive secondary motion and authored task poses; **follow-through and overlap** for hair and arms; **slow in/out** for transitions, never to slide a planted foot; **arcs** for swings and reaches; **secondary action** for thought and attention; **timing** for weight and age; **exaggeration** only enough to read at gameplay distance; **solid drawing** as solid forms and coherent balance; **appeal** through consistent friendly shapes. A teaching source is Disney veteran Jay Jackson's demonstration. This checklist is our application to the rig. [Animation Mentor workshop](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/free-workshop-12-principles-of-animation/).

## Modelling and shading on a phone budget

Valve's character art emphasizes distinctive silhouettes, controlled detail and readable shading. Translate that to Mom's asymmetric bob, Dad's fuller jaw and side part, and Crystal's parted hair and tapered bunches. Shape the cranium, temple, cheek and chin in the existing head surface; retain painted features. Reallocate existing hand vertices to a palm, finger grouping and visible thumb gap. Shape existing hair vertices into broad overlapping locks rather than adding strand meshes. Use large, low-contrast colour changes following those forms, avoiding noisy alternating stripes. Reserve tiny detail for close views. [Mitchell, Francke and Eng, Valve's illustrative-rendering slides](https://cdn.cloudflare.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2_Slides.pdf).

For shading, keep smooth normals across connected head/hair forms and explicit creases at a sole or cuff; close all surfaces. Geometry changes must preserve every outfit and both LODs. The existing one skinned mesh plus shadow stays at two draws. Low/Potato must remain below 3,000 triangles including shadows, which is stricter than the requested +25% cap. Benchmark warmed rig updates separately from software-GPU frame time, and report the host's limitations. No extra per-frame mesh construction, texture uploads, lights or libraries.

## Round 2 — contact, stairs and transitions (4 November 2026)

This pass adds a further fifteen minutes of primary-source research. These are observations and design ideas, not imported code, motion data or assets. Implementation remains original, on three.js r128 without dependencies.

### Stairs: clearance is not a heel kick

[Loverro, Mueske and Hamel, 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3747961/) measured clearance across the whole shoe, rather than treating the toe as a sufficient proxy. The critical part changes with the task: a descending heel can be close to an edge while the toe appears safely clear. Their results support a small, controlled clearance margin, not an exaggerated additional arc above the next tread. For our stylised gait: advance the swing foot as it rises, keep the shin descending towards the ankle, and test the rendered sole against every crossed riser in both directions. A foot-point-only assertion missed the visible problem in round 1. Whole-shoe penetration, support-foot drift and trailing-shin angle are separate acceptance measurements.

### Hands: establish an object frame and a contact window

[Autodesk's IK attachment documentation](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2021/ENU/3DSMax-Character-Animation/files/GUID-ADED19B5-9821-4633-86DF-04C41156EC03.htm) describes changing a limb's attachment between body and object spaces over a defined interval. Our door equivalent is a target on the actual rotating leaf: approach, grasp, follow the first part of the swing, then release while the body continues. A stale point in the closed doorway cannot produce contact after the hinge moves. Reach should be bounded by arm length and a short blend, with no route teleport.

[Animator Shawn Kelly on holding props](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/how-to-animate-your-character-when-hes-holding-a-prop/) explains why the prop's motion and the hand attachment must be designed together; small wrist rotations also produce large changes at distant parts of a prop. The newspaper should therefore have its own reading frame, with two authored grip anchors. Moving the paper to the average of independently posed hands is circular and makes both contacts approximate. For cups and sponges, test the palm, finger pads and object surface, not just the wrist. Reusing existing rig capacity for finger closure is preferable to adding a skeleton or a runtime cloth/hand system.

[Epic's Motion Warping documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-warping-in-unreal-engine) separates a named interaction target from the interval in which an animation accommodates it. The useful inexpensive idea is a limited contact correction. We can apply that to wrist targets while retaining our procedural controller, saved roots and existing family routes.

### Sitting: the chair is a constraint, including during interruption

[COUCH, the authors' 2022 project](https://virtualhumans.mpi-inf.mpg.de/couch/) studies chair interactions conditioned on contact and the scene. The transferable idea is that seat, hands and feet belong to the particular chair, not a universal sitting pose. Our sofa and armchair need independently checked seat height, forward knee room, foot support and standing clearance. On standing, retain the previous support contacts until weight transfers. On interruption, continue from the current blend; on refresh, preserve the presentation phase alongside the existing saved position rather than restarting an unrelated pose. No learned model, dataset or assets are needed for these constraints.

[NVIDIA's 2023 character–scene interaction research](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2023-08_synthesizing-physical-character-scene-interactions) likewise treats carrying and sitting as coordinated interactions with objects. For this budget the lesson is joint validation of body, object and scene, not a physics or machine-learning replacement for the rig.

### Clothes: spend existing polygons where the silhouette bends

[Disney's modelling process](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/modeling/) emphasises readable form, while [its simulation process](https://disneyanimation.com/process/simulation/) keeps secondary movement under artistic control. [Stable Spaces for Real-Time Clothing](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/stable-spaces-for-real-time-clothing/) explores bounded real-time garment behaviour. Our much smaller system should use broad authored elbow/knee/cuff changes on existing rings, not many tiny wrinkle strips. Preserve garment thickness and a clear shoe outsole, heel and toe from all angles. Existing bone deformation supplies the movement; no cloth solver, added draw or per-frame mesh rebuilding is justified.

### Speech, gaze and stationary turns

[Jay Jackson's dialogue advice](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/some-tips-for-animating-dialogue/) starts with the attitude of the body and head, then supports it with selected accents and holds. Our expression and gesture changes should use the existing talk clock and available conversational state; constant symmetrical waving or a permanently moving mouth is not expressiveness. Keep accents small and separated by readable pauses, without modifying the audio system.

[Paul Steed's first-hand December 1999 production article, printed pages 31–33](https://media.gdcvault.com/GD_Mag_Archives/GDM_December_1999.pdf) describes head-leading turns and a procedural foot shuffle to avoid rotating planted feet. This is particularly relevant to our period and budget. Turn the gaze ahead of the chest, choose the outside foot for the requested direction, move only one foot at a time, and verify 45°, 90° and 180° requests separately. On stairs the look target should point along the flight rather than at an incidental nearby viewer. [Ubisoft La Forge's motion-matching overview](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/laforge/news/6xXL85Q3bF2vEj76xmnmIu/introducing-learned-motion-matching) also places foot locking/IK among the final contact corrections; our implementation can retain those inexpensive ideas without adopting its learned system.

### Evidence and budget decisions

A rig probe isolates errors but cannot prove a hand reaches the real sink or that a chair has space behind its back. Round 2 therefore stages the existing production controller in the actual hall, flight, kitchen and lounge, recording camera positions, action phases and door angles with every frame. Desktop and phone use the same world cameras and deterministic model time. Reject an occluded sheet rather than counting it as evidence. Retain native rendering for art and performance captures; any raster reduction used to keep unrelated functional tests timely must be disclosed. Compare the current HEAD, not round 1's older base, and count all outfits and both distance meshes. Reallocate geometry and bones before adding cost.


## Round 3 — locomotion, weight and family appeal (5 November 2026)

This pass starts from the merged 2.70.0 HEAD, not the first-round rig. Research is about principles, observation and production trade-offs only. No source code, rigs, animation clips, models or textures from these references are incorporated. New runtime work remains original JavaScript with the vendored three.js r128 and existing bones/atlas/distance cadence. Modern research dates are documentation, never in-world content.

### Weight transfer into and out of a seat

Gleb Sitnichenko separates a sit into standing, forward inclination, first contact and the supported resting pose. The important distinction for our early Dad frame is that the chest counters the hips moving back; relaxing against the backrest belongs after contact. A support hand can explain how a heavy-looking character controls the descent. The poses must overlap rather than every body part moving toward its final seated target together. [Animation Mentor, sitting tutorial](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-animate-characters-sitting-down/).

Sit-to-stand biomechanics distinguishes initial trunk flexion from momentum transfer and extension. For this game, the useful observation is the ordering: bring the upper body above the feet before extending the legs. Armrest assistance provides a visible support point, but it should not make a healthy parent read as frail. We need one brief, purposeful brace, followed by a release as the legs take weight. Different seat heights/depths need their own contact anchors. These papers are movement references, not medical guidance or physical parameters for the game. [Chair-rise study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6471607/), [sit/stand strategies with external support](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10558536/).

Our adaptation: retain the existing persistent transition state and world foot anchors; stage forward chest rotation, pelvis travel and support-hand reach around that continuous blend. On interruption, evaluate from the current pose rather than restart an anticipation clip. A surface contact is valid only while it is within arm reach. Do not pull an arm into a straight horizontal bar to satisfy an unreachable chair anchor. Test the first 0.10 seconds, the transfer, the settled pose, reverse motion and refresh separately.

### Walking with weight and individual character

Jason Martinsen's walk breakdown emphasises contact, impact, passing and rise, with pelvis rotation, opposing upper-body motion and delayed arm/wrist movement. Starting and stopping require matching travel to the cycle; a pleasing loop alone is insufficient. [Animation Mentor, human walk](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-animating-human-walk-cycle/).

The practical style lesson from Nathaniel Seymour's large/small comparison is to vary timing and overlapping motion, rather than simply changing scale. Our Dad can read heavier through a slower cadence, more measured weight transfer and smaller high-frequency movement; Mom through a brisker stride and confident arm rhythm; the sister through quicker, lighter rise and looser overlap. This is an authored family distinction, not a claim that body size mechanically determines personality. [Animation Mentor, size and movement](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-animating-big-and-small-characters/).

Valve's shipped first-person example, *Half-Life: Alyx*, is especially relevant because nearby characters must stop accurately in tight rooms while keeping foot contact credible. Its SIGGRAPH presentation treats foot contact frames and path constraints as explicit animation data. It also describes selecting motion using distance-based path samples and checking remaining distance at stops. We take the principle that path safety and planted feet constrain the decorative pose; we do not reproduce its motion-matching system or data. [Valve, Character Locomotion in Half-Life: Alyx, 2021](https://media.steampowered.com/apps/valve/2021/Half-Life_Alyx_Locomotion_Slides.pdf).

Epic similarly describes distance-driven pose evaluation for starts, stops and landings. Here the existing procedural gait already advances with travel, and the family follower already accelerates and brakes. That should be audited and tuned rather than replaced with a second competing locomotion system. A speed envelope must still stop at the actual door/body limit and cannot smooth a collision into penetration. [Epic, Distance Matching](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/distance-matching-in-unreal-engine).

### Head stability and quiet idle life

A review of head movement explains coordinated trunk/neck motion during walking: keeping the head useful for orientation does not mean freezing it completely. For our stylised family, oppose some pelvis/chest rotation in the neck and leave a small residual nod; preserve explicit look-at and stair gaze. An exaggerated child bounce should live mostly below the face. [Head movement and its relation to hearing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10338176/).

Shawn Kelly's moving-hold discussion distinguishes a slow settle and small connected ambient motion from arbitrary drifting. A planted idle should shift weight through pelvis, opposing shoulders and a small chest breath. Sparse glances and blinks can punctuate a hold, but simultaneous sinusoidal motion across every joint becomes floaty. Independent seeds and intervals prevent two relatives sharing a visible rhythm. New cosmetic idle/head/hair details should be skipped for far rigs while support/transition logic continues. [Animation Mentor, moving holds](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/why-all-animators-need-to-master-the-moving-hold/).

### Appealing silhouettes and secondary hair

Valve's *Team Fortress 2* art-direction presentation establishes silhouette before interior detail and uses clothing folds to echo the larger forms. The relevant budget lesson is to spend existing rings on neck-to-shoulder flow, rather than adding small details that vanish at two metres. Keep Dad's broad upper body, Mom's bob/cardigan and the sister's bunches/child proportions. Inspect profile, rear and three-quarter views, not just the painted face. [Valve, Stylization With a Purpose, 2008](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2008/GDC2008_StylizationWithAPurpose_TF2.pdf).

Disney's hierarchical hair controls preserve a groom's designed structure while allowing motion at different scales. We cannot afford a strand simulation, but the principle supports a fixed crown and responsive ends/bunches using the two hair bones already present. Turn-driven lag should use the actual animated head heading, remain bounded, and decay after turning. It should never pull a bob away from the skull or erase a recognisable silhouette. [Disney, Hierarchical Controls for Art-Directed Hair, 2018](https://media.disneyanimation.com/uploads/production/publication_asset/175/asset/a13-kaur.pdf).

A secondary-motion breakdown also emphasises that trailing elements continue after the main action settles. Separate left/right responses slightly, retain a short decay and avoid stopping every part on the same frame. Our implementation should remain a tiny scalar response at near distance, not introduce new bones, meshes or per-frame vertex work. [Animation Mentor, secondary motion](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-how-to-animate-secondary-motion/).

### Contact and talking appeal at the player's distance

Shawn Kelly's prop discussion argues for controlling the prop's intended motion and then meeting it with the hands. Round 2 already does this for the newspaper; the remaining check is the actual skinned fingers against both folded faces, and whether the grip is visible from the player's ordinary angle. A hidden palm centre matching an anchor is not sufficient art evidence. [Animation Mentor, holding props](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/how-to-animate-your-character-when-hes-holding-a-prop/).

The *Mitchells* animators describe preserving graphic mouth/eye designs while finding subtle acting within them. This supports keeping our painted atlas rather than adding realistic facial pieces. A dialogue tutorial identifies strong vowel openings and brief closed M/B/P contacts; with only the existing loudness/progress signal, text timing is approximate and must not pretend to be phoneme alignment. Use the playing recording's level as the primary opening signal, close after it ends, and add restrained brow/eye emphasis on selected beats. [Production animator interview](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/behind-the-animation-of-the-mitchells-vs-the-machines/), [dialogue tutorial](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-how-to-animate-believable-lip-sync-for-dialogue-scenes/).

### Acceptance priorities for this pass

First prove the Dad seat silhouette and support contact in the real room, then the folded paper grip, the complete indoor seat inventory and the previously unmatched lounge views. Locomotion/idle/appeal changes must preserve original foot contacts and seat saves, two skinned draws, all outfit variants and the Low/Potato polygon ceiling. Compare paired warmed rig CPU times in the same process; distinguish these from house render time. Native portrait/landscape/tablet sheets assess the player's actual view. A missing test, clipped camera or unmatched frame is a reported gap, never a passing comparison.


The practical mobile constraint is to avoid adding bones/influences for details that cannot be read at the camera distance. Arm's production guidance explicitly makes this trade-off. This pass therefore reuses the existing finger and hair joints, keeps topology/draws fixed, and measures rig CPU separately from rendering. Its old example bone counts are examples, not a universal budget for this game. [Arm, experience with animating for mobile](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/experience-with-animating-for-mobile-devices).

## Round 4 — readable arms, necks and purposeful hands (6 November 2026)

Base: merged 2.72.1, `dbe892526f82b1d10f90ea0cd5976b31070695c1`. This is fresh reading for the fourth pass. References supply observation and design principles only: no downloaded rigs, clips, textures, models or copied implementation. All runtime work remains original code on the vendored three.js r128. Research dates and real production titles belong to these notes, never to the house.

### Appeal is a clear action, not extra anatomical detail

Anthony Wong's posing lesson judges an image by intent, silhouette, the dominant action line and opposing body masses. It demonstrates how an important hand action can disappear inside the torso silhouette. The practical implication here is that a relaxed walker must read as walking before fingers, cloth or facial accents are considered. A bent forearm held across a cardigan conveys carrying even when no object exists. Review the front, profile and rear; a pleasing three-quarter image alone can hide the fault. Keep each person's established proportions and outfit while correcting the misleading action. [Animation Mentor, Building Appealing Character Poses](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-building-appealing-character-poses-for-animation/).

The Pixar/Museum of Science modelling material describes anatomy as an input to the surface form and shows a complete turntable through construction stages. Our inference is to spend existing vertices where the silhouette changes: the head-to-neck-to-shoulder connection deserves them more than small surface decorations. This does not justify importing a film model or adding realistic facial components to our painted faces. [The Science Behind Pixar, Modeling](https://sciencebehindpixar.org/pipeline/modeling).

### Arm swing: shoulder first, quiet elbow and wrist

Rusty Animator's authored walk demonstration treats the shoulder, elbow and wrist as a connected pendulum with staggered extrema. It also checks the arm arc from the front after establishing the profile swing. The transferable idea is slight overlap, not its example frame numbers, software controls or animation curves. For our family, retain the individual shoulder amplitudes and opposing torso rotation, then reduce the excessive forward-end elbow fold. A soft elbow should not turn every forward swing into a lifting gesture. Measure the actual upper/forearm angle and wrist height relative to the pelvis through complete cycles, including starting, braking and the free arm during carrying. [Rusty Animator, walk-cycle demonstration](https://rustyanimator.com/animation-walk-cycle/).

### A neck connects the skull to a shoulder mass

Stan Prokopenko's form lesson explains a forward-inclined neck axis and the different front/back heights of its connection. His mannequin lesson explicitly includes the trapezius connection into the rear of the head. These are useful structural cues without adding tendons, an exaggerated Adam's apple or realistic muscle strips. Our adaptation is a broader base tucked into the shirt, a gentler exposed throat, a slightly forward upper section, and a shoulder slope that flows into it. Simply enlarging a vertical tube preserves the tube problem. [Proko, Neck Muscles — Form](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-neck-muscles-form), [Mannequinization](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/mannequinization-structure-of-the-human-body).

Check the collar at the same time. A wider skin section inside an unchanged collar can intersect, while a detached collar can produce a bright gap on turns. Reallocate the existing neck/torso rings and preserve overlap and smooth normals across every outfit. Inspect a close profile, rear and front, plus the complete figure at four metres. These are shape guidelines for a stylised family, not medical or biomechanical instructions.

### Relaxed hands, support hands and grips are different poses

Thom Roberts' hand-posing lesson starts with a gentle curved fan, imperfect alignment and restrained distal-joint curl. Kelly Vawter's related lesson uses contrast and grouped fingers to make an organic gesture. For this rig, the existing four rounded finger forms and reserved flex joints are enough to improve the read; extra articulated finger chains are not justified. A resting palm should settle onto a thigh or armrest, with fingers following the surface and a small left/right difference. Avoid identical cupped palms hovering together in front of the lap. A push uses a flatter palm and a brief stronger contact; a carrying grip closes around a specific handle. [AnimSchool, Hand Posing Basics](https://blog.animschool.edu/2025/05/09/hand-posing-basics/), [Posing Hands for Animation](https://blog.animschool.edu/2025/06/13/posing-hands-for-animation-2/).

Nathaniel Seymour's gesture lesson connects hand shape to intention, wrist arcs and the rest of the upper body. Its warning about symmetric, flat hands is especially relevant to our resting poses. Our inexpensive adaptation is a small set of authored contact frames, grouped finger closure and restrained asymmetry. Measure finger/palm contact against the actual solid object, then inspect whether the grip reads from the player's distance; a correct wrist point hidden behind a prop is insufficient. [Animation Mentor, Hand Gestures for Dialogue](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-how-to-animate-hand-gestures/).

### Props retain their own frame when contact changes

Autodesk's parent-constraint workflow demonstrates an object passing between the hand and a stationary surface while maintaining its offset. The useful idea is continuity of the object's transform at release. Our newspaper must not derive a new orientation from hands that have already begun a conversational gesture. Cache its last authored object frame, then perform any lowering/release from that frame. A page action should move a real solid leaf about its fold, using existing rig capacity if possible. No billboard page, copied constraint code or new physics system is needed. [Autodesk, parent-constraint workflow](https://download.autodesk.com/global/docs/maya2013/en_US/files/CSCo_Parent_constraint_workflow.htm).

Magazine, pad, crayon, can and briefcase require separate contact decisions. A thin edge is held between opposing finger/thumb surfaces; a crayon is a pinch rather than a handle grip; a can and case hang from their handles. Author each object frame and wrist offset together. Keep a stable supporting hand while the other performs the small action. Do not solve a two-handed prop by repeatedly moving it to an average of two independently animated wrists.

### Performance, persistence and acceptance

Use the current skeleton, mesh count, painted atlas and distance cadence. Prefer cached action targets over allocating fresh arrays and objects every frame. Disable extra expression/page detail at distance. Rig CPU measurements exclude rendering and must be paired against this HEAD; software captures do not establish physical-phone GPU performance.

The fresh no-repeat memory belongs to speech selection, not to posing. Facial interpretation may consume the existing line, loudness and progress; it must neither choose a line nor alter hearing history. Questions can lift brows near an audible accent, sentence endings can receive one small nod, and an addressed person can maintain player gaze. Text/progress supplies approximate phrase structure, not phoneme timing. Preserve silence and end-of-recording mouth closure.

Acceptance order follows the owner's review: fix the arm/neck read first, then resting/support hands and newspaper continuity, then exercise stair interruptions and actual page reloads. Record remaining prop/acting gaps honestly if the time reserve prevents a finished implementation. Native before/after cameras, actual skinned contact tests and every requested regression are separate forms of evidence; none substitutes for another.

The grasp-taxonomy paper distinguishes contact opposition and thumb use rather than treating every hand-held object alike. It also explicitly excludes gravity-dependent hooks and flat-hand supports from its chosen static-grasp definition. That limitation matters here: a briefcase hook and a resting thigh palm should not be judged as though both were precision pinches. We take this distinction as an authoring aid, not a complete anatomical hand controller. [Feix and colleagues, A Comprehensive Grasp Taxonomy](https://www.csc.kth.se/grasp/taxonomyGRASP.pdf).
